# Notes for the agent: what the data and the logic turned out to be

The idiosyncrasies the first build found, for whoever changes a ledger builder or the engine next. CLAUDE.md is the map; this is the running list of surprises. Add to it whenever a gate or a reader's correction teaches us one. The research that chose the sources (five Opus probes and two factoid corpora, 2026-10-21/22) is under `research/`, each README with its numbers.

## The spine (coaches and teams)

- **Two sources by era, one spine.** 1869–1935 is James Howell's team pages (`data/history/games-1869-1935.json.gz`, 20,849 games, every game of every team he lists as major, read from both teams' pages and keyed by the date and the pair so a neutral-site game, which reads "vs." on both pages, is one game; 0 score disagreements). From 1936 the spine is Streak King's payload (63,975 games to date), which has the AP rank of both teams at kickoff (-1 before a season's first poll, 0 unranked), the neutral/conference/postseason flags, Repole's errata and the coach stints (`teams[].hc` = `[coachIdx, startSeason, startOrd, interim]`, CFBD ids in `coachIds`). The payload's `ep` is the local game date as an epoch day; ESPN's dates are UTC.
- **"Major" is Howell's list** (`byconf.htm` spans, with Streak King's `fbs-span-bridges.json` so a wartime gap doesn't end a span). A program's ledger is its major seasons only; a coach's games are his team-games in those seasons. So Washington's 1908–15 (Gil Dobie's 58-0-3 unbeaten run) is off the spine (Howell lists Washington from 1916), Boise State's D-II years are out, and Brian Kelly's Grand Valley State, Jim Tressel's Youngstown State and Bobby Bowden's Samford years are not here. 47% of pre-1936 games are against non-major opponents (high schools and athletic clubs among them before 1900; `F_MAJOROPP` says).
- **Before 1880 the games weren't eleven-man football**, and a third of the 1870s rows are ones Howell marks unverified. They are in the ledger; the era picker's 1880 cut takes them out. Scoring values settled around 1912; a points board over the 1880s reads 835 for Harvard 1886.
- **The coach of record.** From 1936, the payload's stints resolved game by game (that repo's `coach-overrides.json` rules the mid-season changes). Before 1936, Wikipedia's "<Program> football coach navbox" templates (`data/ref/coaches-1869-1935.json`, `scripts/build-coaches-pre1936.mjs` from the probe's downloads), one coach per team-season: a season with two coaches in turn or co-coaches has no coach of record (111 team-seasons), "No coach" seasons none (489), and 3.6% of major team-games 1869–2026 have none. CFBD's `/coaches` 1869–1935 would be the matching source and couldn't be fetched (the API's monthly quota was spent); the navboxes agree with CFBD's coach in all 7,328 team-seasons from 1936 where both name one. A coach whose career crosses 1936 is joined to his CFBD id by name (584 team-seasons); the rest carry `cw:<name>` ids. The file is CC BY-SA 4.0, as Wikipedia's license asks, and Method says so.
- **On-field results, always.** Vacated wins stand (Paterno's 111, Bowden's 12, Saban's 5 of 2007, Kelly's 21), forfeits are as played (Florida State–Tulane 1983), acting-coach games go to the coach of record the payload names (Michigan 2023 is all Harbaugh's). Method words every one. The NCAA counts a tie as half a win in a percentage (Camp 69 vs our 68) and lists George Woodruff's 100th win in game 109 where Howell's Penn pages reach it in 111.
- **Ranks**: `rk`/`ork` are 0 unranked, 1–25, 255 unknown (before 1936, and before a season's first poll until the preseason poll began in 1950). The rank stats read UNKNOWN there and sit the entity out of the window (`board.partial`), which is why a 1936–49 coach's "vs ranked" window often sits out: his September games had no poll.

## The player ledger

- **ESPN's box scores, 2004 on**, from the sportsdataverse `espn_cfb_player_box` release: one row per player per stat category per game (`category`: passing, rushing, receiving, interceptions, puntReturns, kicking, punting from 2004; kickReturns from 2009; fumbles from 2016; defensive full only from 2024). Use the plain `.csv` assets (named columns in every season, the running season included); the `.csv.gz` ones were last built in July 2026 and carry `stat_1..5` for passing rows without QBR, but also a few real rows the csv dropped (18 in 2004, 9 in 2005), so the builder unions them. Team-total rows (" Team", negative ids, about 1,000 a season from 2016) are skipped. A category a season lacks for most games is unknown in every game of that season (`statSince` in the ledger; `querySince` reads it), measured against the latest season's share of games with a row, since a game with no interception has no interception row.
- **Holes**: four 2006 Hawai'i home games (UNLV, Eastern Illinois, Nevada, Idaho) don't exist at ESPN at all, so Colt Brennan reads 42 touchdown passes for 2006 (the NCAA has 58); Texas Tech at UTEP 2007-09-08 is missing, which leaves Graham Harrell 48 completions and Michael Crabtree 15 catches short; USC–Washington State 2005 has no athletes. 113 team-games on the spine have no ESPN box; the season windows count them as gaps (the team's game number comes from the spine's schedule, matched by ESPN id and date ±1). Depth players' receiving rows are missing inside games before 2008 (12.6% of 2004 team-games are 20+ yards short on receiving vs passing; 0.3% by 2008); stars survive.
- **Sacks count against a quarterback's rushing yards** (the NCAA's convention, and ESPN's: Harrell's 2007 rushing is -83 here, -91 in the record book with the missing game). Passing yards are gross. The gate pins both.
- **Positions**: ESPN's rosters are "Unknown" for every player before 2014, and the roster's class, height, weight and birth date are the player's latest profile, not the season's. A position is the roster's modal group (QB; RB/FB/HB/TB; WR; TE; DEF for every defensive abbreviation; K; P), else one read off the box score (30+ attempts and more attempts than carries → QB; 20+ carries → RB; 10+ catches → WR, tight ends included; kicks, punts, tackles), `posSrc` says which; 7,665 players have none and count only in "players".
- **Censoring**: a player whose first ESPN season is 2004 and whose career ran fewer than four seasons from it may have played in 2003 and sits out the career fields (2,875 of them, Adrian Peterson and Reggie Bush among them; Peterson was a 2004 freshman and needs a receipt in a ruling file to come back); four seasons from 2004 prove a 2004 start. Their seasons still rank on the season boards.
- **FBS only**: a game counts when one side is major that season (by the spine's spans through the ESPN id); an FCS team's players are not in the ledger even for that game, but an FBS player's game against an FCS team is (the NCAA counts it).
- **Dates**: ESPN's `game_date` is UTC, so a Saturday night game is dated Sunday; the local date is the spine's where the game joins, else the UTC date less ten hours. 372 schedule games didn't join the spine (FBS-vs-FCS games the spine has under a different pairing, mostly).
- **Ids**: ESPN athlete ids are stable across seasons and schools (Burrow is 3915511 at Ohio State and LSU); the probe found the same name and team under two ids 0–26 times a season, which a merge list would fix.
- **The 2026 file** is updated weekly (a Saturday's games arrive by Sunday) and old games aren't re-pulled.

## The logic

- Four sequences: a career's games, its tenures (contiguous stints at one team, `stints()`), its seasons, and a team's games of a season (the team's schedule on the spine, so a missed or missing game is a gap the window still counts; a mid-season change reads on the first team's schedule). A tenure row's key is `<id>:t<k>`, a season row's `<id>:<season>`.
- A finished season, career or stint fills a longer window whole (the words say "all 548 of his games"); an active one is still inside it. Without this no finished career could rank on a "most X in a career" board.
- The program mine takes season windows and milestones only (`shapesFor('team')`): "through its first 1,360 games" is a claim nobody makes.
- `reach` reuses `n` as the target and `every` ranks by the count; competition ranks with ties; crossings are entries into a tie or a lead; `toMatch` is arithmetic.
- `queryCols(q)` names the columns a query reads; `evaluate` throws if one is missing, and the page loads them first (`ensureCols`), keeping the previous board on screen meanwhile. A kind switch loads that kind's ledger the same way.

## Working here

- `rtk` rewrites vitest's output and its parser fails on it: `rtk proxy npx vitest run --reporter=verbose`.
- Node 22 strips types, so `scripts/*.mjs` import `src/lib/*.ts` directly; `scripts/lib/ledger-node.mjs` loads a whole ledger for a script.
- The ledger build takes about a minute for the players (23 seasons of csv in `data/raw`, 300 MB); the coach and team ledgers are seconds. The player mine takes minutes; the coach and team mines seconds.
- `npm run typecheck` is two passes (the root, then `workers/share`): the DOM lib and the Workers types collide on `CacheStorage`.
- `wrangler` and `opentype.js` are pinned (4.151.3 and 1.3.4); Georgia Bold comes from the Mac and is never committed; CI's cards fall back to Graduate.
- A named board's path is served by GitHub Pages as a directory index; on the dev server Vite's SPA fallback serves `index.html` and `App.jsx` reads the slug from `window.location.pathname` in `main.jsx`, never in render.
- The dev server is `.claude/launch.json`'s `cfb-comparator`, port 5178; preview 4175; worker 8789.
